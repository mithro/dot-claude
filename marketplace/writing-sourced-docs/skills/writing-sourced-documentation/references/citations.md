# Citations: the house style and why

## The form

```markdown
The vendor lists a model X on its capabilities page.[^vend-01] Two sources at once.[^doc-04][^txt-02]

<!-- footnotes -->

[^vend-01]: Vendor Inc., *Facilities & Capabilities*, accessed
    2026-08-30. <https://www.example.com/capabilities/>
[^doc-04]: Project Authors, *stack.svg* (published stack drawing),
    project repository. <https://example.org/docs/stack.svg>
```

1. **Inline citation = footnote reference** `[^label]`, directly after the claim, quotation or
   number it supports, consistently after the full stop. Several sources: `[^a][^b]`, no
   separators. A marker at the end of a lead-in sentence that introduces a list or table
   supports every item: keep it on the lead-in (before the colon) or in the table's first
   header cell, never on the last row only, which leaves the other rows looking uncited.
2. **Labels** are lower-case ASCII letters, digits and hyphens, and stable across the whole
   site: the same source has the same label on every page. Use the site's source-inventory
   key where one exists (`DOC-04` → `[^doc-04]`); otherwise `author-year` (`[^chew-2002]`),
   `pat-…` for patents, `wiki-…` for Wikipedia. Stable labels are what let a checker confirm
   every page cites only inventoried sources, and let a reader recognise a source across pages.
3. **Definitions** hold the full citation: authors, title, venue or publisher, date,
   identifier (DOI, patent number, ISBN) and the URL in angle brackets; continuation lines
   indented four spaces; all definitions at the very end of the page under a
   `<!-- footnotes -->` comment. A page repeats the full citation of every source it uses so
   it can be read on its own; the inventory holds exactly one entry per key.
4. **Every definition referenced, every reference defined.** Sphinx warns on an orphan, and
   the build runs with warnings as errors; a deleted duplicate sentence is the usual cause.
5. **No reference-style links** (`[text]: url`) and no bare URLs in prose. An inline link is
   only a convenience copy of a keyed footnote: `[head](<URL>)`, angle brackets mandatory so
   a URL with parentheses stays valid, the URL copied character for character from the first
   URL of that page's own definition. A URL that exists only inline escapes the link checker,
   which scans footnote definitions and the inventory.
6. **Retrieval dates** on anything that changes: capability lists, job postings, product
   pages, dealer listings ("accessed 2026-08-30"). Search-engine snippets and pages never
   retrieved are not cited or mentioned.
7. **Fetch before citing**, every URL and DOI. Never from memory.

## Generated data pages are the exception

A page generated by a script from a curated dataset (a patent index, a paper index) is a data
listing, not prose: it cites inline in its tables and carries no reading tiers. Say so in the
style guide so the checkers skip those directories deliberately rather than by accident.

## Dead links

A URL is "dead" after it fails in two runs at least 24 h apart with 404, 410, DNS failure, 5xx
or timeout, on a host not known to block scripts. One failure is not dead.

1. Find a snapshot (Wayback availability API, timestamp = the citation's retrieval date;
   polite user agent, one request per few seconds; retry once, then the other scheme, then
   with and without `www.`, then the CDX endpoint). Accept only an available `200`.
2. Verify it: fetch the snapshot once and confirm the title, or a string the page quotes, is
   present. A captured soft-404 or a redirect is no snapshot.
3. Cite both, archive first, in every definition that repeats the URL and in the inventory:

```markdown
[^key]: Author, *Title*, publisher, date.
    <https://web.archive.org/web/20260411150120/https://example.com/page>
    (Wayback Machine capture of 2026-04-11; original, dead since 2026-09-19:
    `https://example.com/page`).
```

   The archive URL comes first because the linked head and the hover card take the first
   URL. The original goes in backticks: searchable, not a link, so the checker stops
   reporting it. Use one fixed wording ("Wayback Machine capture of YYYY-MM-DD").
4. No snapshot anywhere (try archive.today by hand too): remove verbatim quotations from the
   source, rest no claim on it alone, keep it at most as a dated mention ("retrieved
   YYYY-MM-DD, no longer online, not archived") with strength "weak" beside a retrievable
   source or a marked inference. Apply this on every page that cites it.
5. A DOI whose landing page is broken keeps the DOI plus a dated note. Never replace a DOI
   with an archive URL.

## Quotations

* Verbatim, inside quotation marks, with the source's own units, spelling and capitals.
* Cut with "[…]" inside the marks; an ellipsis the source itself prints stays as printed.
* Never split a sentence inside a quotation; split before or after it, and never add or remove
  a quotation mark. A sentence whose only split point lies inside a quotation stays whole and
  is listed.
* A sentence may not open on a lowercase quotation; the lead-in added is neutral ("The patent
  adds:"), never a relation ("Its consequence:", "Similarly,").
* A quick-facts cell may paraphrase a quotation only when the exact words survive, with their
  marker, in the body.
* The subject's own entries (a capability list's strings) always keep their quotation marks:
  that is what tells the reader what the subject said from what this page infers.

## The three reading tiers

Under `## References`, three annotated lists:

* **Cross-check** — primary public sources that can verify a specific claim on this page: the
  subject's own documentation and statements, regulatory filings, qualification reports,
  patents, vendor data.
* **High-level understanding** — encyclopaedia articles, textbooks, tutorials, talks.
* **Deep dive** — peer-reviewed papers, review articles, theses, patents from several
  assignees, book chapters, standards, application notes, course notes. Minimum eight entries
  on a leaf page, twelve on a class, overview or index page, drawn from several kinds of
  source. Prefer sources the reader can open (DOI or URL).

Each bullet is short: `[head](<URL>) — one clause saying what the reader gets.[^label]`. The
head is the text before the first " — "; when a role or backtick precedes an italic title,
link only the title. With N markers and N italic titles in the same order, link title *i* to
marker *i*'s URL. Never invent a URL; never use one that the page's own definitions do not
carry. The count of `* ` bullets under *Deep dive* is checked, so never merge or split them in
a formatting pass.

## Evidence strength

For claims that a specific organisation owns or uses something:

| Grade | Means |
|---|---|
| **strong** | the organisation's own statement, or its vendor's, naming it |
| **medium** | a job posting, an interview with a named employee, a dated listing |
| **weak** | indirect evidence |
| **inference** | this page's reasoning from the above |

Grade two things separately and say the reason for each: *Tool exists:* strong (capability
list) · *Runs this step:* inference (the only listed tool of that kind). A capability list
never names a step, so the assignment is always at best an inference. Never normalise grade
words across pages in a formatting pass; they are the writer's judgement.

## Restricted content

Some sites have content a reader must be able to avoid (in the worked example, passages from
patents that may still be in force, under the owner's rule; elsewhere it might be spoilers,
licensed excerpts or material under embargo). Such content sits only inside a collapsed block
with a fixed title stating why it is collapsed and its status. Nothing from inside it —
number, title, value, phrase — appears outside: not in a summary box, a table, a caption, a
figure or the open prose. The footnote definition and the inventory entry may stay open but
carry the same status sentence, compared character for character with the dataset that
records the status. A checker flattens the page and matches every spelling of the
identifier, long titles and distinctive phrases. See `worked-example-sky130.md` for the
patent instantiation.
